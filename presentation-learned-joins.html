01 · The problem

No join algorithm wins for every pair of datasets

Distribution

Skew and geometry distribution affect the amount of candidate work.

Existing partitions

Whether and how inputs are partitioned changes the cost of a join.

Input relationship

Individual summaries alone may miss how the two datasets overlap spatially.

Goal: make a useful planning decision without executing every candidate join.

02 · My contribution

Learn estimates and choices from spatial data

Initial optimizer

I developed models to estimate join cardinality and candidate comparisons and to guide the choice of join algorithm.

Extended framework

I extended the work to choose a partitioning strategy and tune the number of partitions and plane-sweep direction.

The framework uses features describing each input and the relationship between the two inputs.

04 · Modeling

Represent both the data and the algorithm

Dataset summaries

Features include dataset characteristics and spatial skew.

Joint distribution

Features based on combined histograms capture relationships between inputs.

Comparison work

Separate algorithm-specific models predict geometric comparisons, a hardware-independent measure of work.

Result cardinality and candidate comparisons answer different questions: output size alone is not the whole cost.

05 · Tradeoffs

Cheap features must preserve useful information

Summary versus detail

Compact summaries are cheaper than exhaustive execution, but can lose information about fine-grained spatial structure.

Prediction versus execution

Comparison counts describe an important part of work, while actual runtime can also depend on shuffle, storage, and hardware.

A learned choice is a prediction, not a guarantee of the fastest plan for every dataset.

06 · Evaluation

Test on both synthetic and real spatial data

Experiments

The research compares models against baselines on large-scale synthetic and real datasets.

Scope

The 2024 evaluation uses point and rectangle datasets and an intersection join; results should be read in that experimental context.

Datasets, code, and baseline models were released to support further research.

07 · Further directions

Connect learned estimates to production feedback

Generalization

Evaluate on distributions and geometry types beyond the training workload.

Plan safety

Explore confidence-aware fallbacks when predictions are uncertain.

Feedback

Compare estimated work with observed execution and detect distribution drift.

These are possible directions, not claims about the existing implementation.
