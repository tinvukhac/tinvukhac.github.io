01 · The problem

The right partitioner depends on the data and the goal

Distribution

Uniform, clustered, and skewed datasets can favor different techniques.

Quality metric

Overlap, partition shape, and load balance capture different priorities.

Selection cost

Physically partitioning the full input with every candidate just to pick one is expensive.

Goal: predict a suitable technique for the input and chosen metric using a compact summary.

02 · My contribution

Learn the relationship between distribution and partition quality

Offline training

I generated varied datasets, evaluated six partitioning techniques against four quality metrics, and trained models on summaries and quality results.

Application

For a new dataset and selected quality metric, the trained model predicts a partitioning technique without running all candidates on that input.

The accompanying code supports data preparation, model training, and applying a selector with Beast.

04 · Partition quality

Different metrics reward different properties

Area and margin

Capture aspects of the spatial extent and shape of partitions.

Overlap

Reflects how much partition regions overlap, which can affect query work.

Cardinality variation

Measures differences in partition sizes, relevant to balancing work.

A technique selected for one metric need not be the best choice for another.

05 · Tradeoffs

A summary is cheaper than an exhaustive search, but not free

Summary fidelity

Histograms and skew measures compress the dataset; details lost in that summary may affect the prediction.

Training coverage

New distributions may differ from generated training data, and the chosen quality metric is a proxy for the eventual workload.

The selector predicts a technique; it does not guarantee the optimal result for every unseen dataset.

06 · Evaluation

Compare prediction accuracy with a baseline

Data

Experiments use synthetic and real spatial datasets to evaluate the selector.

Result

The paper reports better partitioner-selection accuracy than its baseline; the comparison is specific to its datasets, metrics, and models.

Prediction accuracy and end-to-end query performance are related but distinct measures.
