Incremental spatial partitioning

Maintaining distributed spatial data as records are inserted and deleted.

01 · The problem

Spatial datasets change, but full rebuilds are expensive

Block-level analytics

Distributed file-system blocks support large analytical scans, but traditional static partitioning does not naturally handle ongoing updates.

Record-level structures

Other systems support individual updates and selective queries, yet make different tradeoffs for large analytical workloads.

Goal: support bulk insertions and deletions without reorganizing the entire dataset after every update.

02 · My contribution

Selectively reorganize partitions under a budget

Formalize

I formulated partition optimization for incremental spatial data and showed that the problem is NP-hard.

Estimate

I developed a block-based model to estimate range-query processing costs as partitions change.

Implement

I implemented and compared three strategies for selecting partitions to reorganize.

03 · Framework

Separate selection from physical reorganization

01

Receive updates

Handle batches of inserted and deleted records.

02

Assess partitions

Consider existing partitions and the effect of changes on query cost.

03

Select

Choose partitions for reorganization within the available budget.

04

Reorganize

Physically reorganize selected data in distributed file-system blocks.

Selection and physical reorganization are separate subproblems; the framework does not need to rebuild every partition.

04 · Partition selection

Three strategies, different decisions

R*-tree inspired

Uses ideas from R*-tree insertion and splitting to guide partition maintenance.

LSM-tree inspired

Applies an LSM-style merge policy to maintain partitions.

Cost based

Uses the range-query cost model to choose reorganization under the budget.

The framework makes the tradeoff explicit: spend update work now to improve future query performance.

05 · Evaluation

Measure maintenance and query performance together

What was measured

Experiments on large real datasets evaluated partitioning time, query throughput, and partition quality.

What the comparison shows

The paper compares the proposed strategies with each other and with existing approaches; reported outcomes depend on the workload and budget.

A lower maintenance cost is useful only if the resulting query performance remains acceptable.

06 · Further directions

Adapt reorganization to changing workloads

Workload awareness

Explore how observed query patterns could influence future reorganization choices.

Changing data

Study how distribution drift and bursty updates affect the maintenance budget.

Validation

Compare estimated query costs with observed costs across workloads.

These are possible directions, not claims about the existing implementation.
